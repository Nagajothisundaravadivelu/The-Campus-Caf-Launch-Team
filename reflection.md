# Reflection on agent collaboration

The team divides work that would otherwise ask one model to research, calculate, persuade and approve its own claims. The Market Researcher is instructed to return source URLs and evidence limits, so broad consumer or household evidence should not quietly become a claim about campus students. The Financial Analyst works from explicit planning inputs and uses the calculator tool, making the break-even logic inspectable. The Sustainability Officer separately checks whether environmental or plant-based claims have relevant support and suggests a measurable pilot action.

The Marketing Writer turns those outputs into a short decision brief. The Skeptical Reviewer then receives the original research, financial analysis, sustainability audit and draft as explicit task context. That independent pass is useful for checking unsupported claims, source quality, arithmetic, pricing assumptions and omitted risks. In particular, it helps keep the recommendation conditional: an eight-week pilot is a way to test demand, not evidence that the illustrative target will be reached.

This collaboration improves traceability and gives the reviewer a better chance to catch errors, but it cannot guarantee accurate interpretation. Search results may be stale or geographically irrelevant, and generated citations still need spot-checking. The financial model remains only as good as its assumptions; verified campus footfall, student feedback, local prices and supplier quotes should replace them before committing funds. Startup costs, taxes and other exclusions also matter. A failed search or calculator call should stop the run and be shown clearly, rather than allowing the team to produce an unsupported success-shaped brief.
